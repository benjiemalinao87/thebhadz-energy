# ops/interview-scripts.md — homeowner story interviews

**Created:** 2026-07-28 · **Owner:** _[founder name]_ · Cadence: ≥1 per founder per week (§1.7)

> Discovery interviews are **casual conversations, not sales calls** (§2). The only ask
> permitted at the end is an introduction to another homeowner. Nothing said here enters
> the pipeline — an interview is not a lead. Do not pitch. Do not mention price. If they
> ask what you do, answer in one sentence and go back to their story.
>
> **Write the snapshot within 24h with exact quotes, filed in `ops/tree.md`.**
> An interview not written up did not happen.

## The three banned questions

Never ask, in any language, any version of:

- ❌ "Would you buy solar?" / "Bibili ka ba ng solar?"
- ❌ "Would you pay ₱99,500 for this?" / "Magkano ang handa mong bayaran?"
- ❌ "Do you like this idea?" / "Maganda ba ito sa tingin mo?"

All three produce polite lies. Filipino politeness makes the false positive worse, not
better. Ask what already happened instead — the past is the only thing people report
accurately.

## Scoring what you hear (§2)

Write one of these next to every statement in your notes:

| Tag | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **COMPLIMENT** | Praise. Worth zero. | "Ang galing niyo!" |
| **FLUFF** | Generic, hypothetical, future-tense. Zero. | "Sana magkaroon kami niyan someday." |
| **IDEA** | A feature request. Note it, don't act. | "Dapat may app kayo." |
| **FACT** | Something that actually happened. **Data.** | "Last month ₱9,400 ang bill namin." |
| **COMMITMENT** | Time, reputation or money given. **Data.** | "Punta kayo Saturday, ipapakita ko yung bill." |

A meeting that produced only compliments and fluff is a **failed meeting**. Log it as one.

## Script 1 — On a stopgap (genset / UPS / just enduring it)

**Who:** has no solar, is already spending money or effort on the power problem.
**This is the highest-value tier.** Prior spend is the proof the pain is real.

1. "Kumusta yung kuryente dito lately?" — let them talk, don't steer.
2. **"Tell me about the last brownout. When was it, what were you doing?"**
3. "What did the household do while it was out?" (Who was affected? Kids studying? Business?)
4. "Did anything spoil, break, or get cancelled?" — pushes toward real cost.
5. **"What have you already bought or done because of it?"** — genset, AVR, UPS, extra batteries, rewiring, nothing.
   - If they bought something: **"How much was it, and when? Where did you buy it? Do you still use it?"**
   - If nothing: **"What stopped you?"** — the honest answer is usually price, trust, or not knowing who to call.
6. "Anong gas/fuel bill niyo per month para dito?" (if genset)
7. **"Can I see last month's BILECO bill?"** — ask to see the actual paper. If they show it, that is a COMMITMENT. Photograph it with permission.
8. "Nag-iba ba yung bill over the last year? Ano ang ginawa niyo tungkol dito?"
9. "Sino pa dito sa inyo ang ganito ang problema?" → **ask for the intro.**

## Script 2 — Considered solar and did not push through

**Who:** has had a quote or seriously looked into it. Tells you exactly what kills deals.

1. "Kailan mo unang naisip mag-solar? Anong nangyari?"
2. **"Who did you talk to, and what did they quote you?"** — get the number and the company. This is corridor evidence.
3. **"What happened after that quote?"** — let them narrate. Do not suggest reasons.
4. "Ano yung pinaka-nag-alala sa'yo?" — then shut up. The first hesitation is usually the real one.
5. **"What would have had to be true for you to push through?"** — past-conditional about a real event, not a hypothetical purchase.
6. "May kilala ka bang nagpakabit na? Anong sabi nila?" — reveals local proof (or its absence).
7. "Sino pa ang nag-quote sa'yo?" → intro ask.

**Listen hardest for:** price shape (not just amount), scam fear, roof/ownership problems,
"walang installer dito," and whether the quote ever arrived in writing at all.

## Script 3 — Never considered it

**Who:** the largest tier. Tells you what the category even means to people here.

1. "Kapag naririnig mo 'solar,' ano yung naiisip mo?" — record the exact words. This is your positioning input.
2. "May kilala ka bang may solar? Anong alam mo tungkol sa kanila?"
3. **"Magkano ang bill niyo last month?"** — then: "Ano ang pinakamalaking gumagamit?"
4. "Kung tumaas yung bill, ano ang unang ginagawa niyo?" — past behaviour, phrased as routine.
5. "Anong pinakamalaking gastos sa bahay ngayong taon?" — where does power actually rank?
6. **"Ano ang pinakahuling malaking binili niyo para sa bahay? Paano niyo napagdesisyunan?"** — this is the real question: how a purchase of this size gets decided in this household, and who decides.
7. Intro ask.

## After every interview — the 24h snapshot

Copy this into `ops/tree.md`:

```
DATE / NAME / BARANGAY / TIER (1 stopgap · 2 refused · 3 never considered)
Last brownout, in their words:
Monthly bill (₱, and did you see it? Y/N):
Already spent on the problem (what, ₱, when):
Exact quote #1 (verbatim, in their language):
Exact quote #2:
Tags: COMPLIMENT / FLUFF / IDEA / FACT / COMMITMENT — count each
Intro given? (name + how to reach)
What I believed before this that is now wrong:
```

That last line is the point of the whole exercise. If it is blank three interviews in a
row, you are pitching rather than listening.
