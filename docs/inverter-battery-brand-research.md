# Inverter & battery brand research — Solis, SolaX, Pylontech, Dyness

**Researched:** 2026-07-26 · **Scope:** pricing, locations, partnership routes
**Purpose:** Model B (SC-05/SC-06) supplier selection — what we buy, from whom, at what
landed cost, and what a supply relationship with each brand realistically looks like for a
pre-revenue installer.

## How to read the confidence markers

Prices move fast and most PH solar pricing lives behind quote forms. Every figure below
carries a marker:

| Marker | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **[V]** | Verified from the manufacturer's own site or a primary/trade source, dated. |
| **[S]** | Secondary source (PH solar content site, marketplace aggregator). Directionally useful, not quotable to a customer. |
| **[U]** | Unverified / could not be confirmed. Treat as a question to ask a supplier, not a number. |

**Nothing in this document is a quote.** Every price here needs a written supplier quote
before it enters a BOM or a customer-facing number.

## 0. Executive summary — the five things that matter

1. **Solis is the only one of the four with a physical Philippine presence.** Solis lists a
   Metro Manila office and a local service phone/email on its own website **[V]**. SolaX,
   Pylontech and Dyness reach the Philippines only through third-party distributors. For a
   startup whose table stakes include *warranty that gets honored* (CLAUDE.md §3), a local
   service line is worth more than a ₱/unit saving.
2. **Dyness has the cleanest local channel.** Photonergy Inc. (Antipolo, Rizal) publicly
   states it is the Official Philippine Distributor for Dyness **[V]**, with a second PH
   seller (Blueshift Energy, Quezon City) also stocking Powerbox Pro **[V]**. That is a real
   phone number we can call this week.
3. **The cheap China-direct prices are a trap, not a saving.** The US$630–1,099 Solis and
   US$912–1,099 SolaX FOB prices found are from **Chinese trading companies, not the
   manufacturers** **[V]**. Buying that way puts serial numbers outside the authorized
   channel and puts the manufacturer warranty at risk — which breaks a table-stakes
   requirement to save maybe 20%. See §6.
4. **No manufacturing/OEM partnership is realistically available to us at zero installs**,
   and Dyness is the only one of the four that even has a visible door for it ("Brand" is a
   selectable partnership type on their partner form **[V]**). See §7 for the staged ladder
   and §8 for the Founder-OS gate this trips.
5. **The battery is what squeezes the ₱99,500 flagship.** ILAW/SANDIGAN are specced at
   2.5–3 kWh against a ₱62–86k cost target (market.html §5). The smallest genuine modules
   from these brands — Pylontech US2000C (2.4 kWh), Dyness B4850 (2.4 kWh) — are the only
   SKUs that fit, and we do not yet have a verified landed peso price for either. **Getting
   those two numbers is the single highest-value supplier call we can make.**

## 6. The grey-market trap — read before anyone quotes a China-direct price

The lowest prices found in this research were:

- Solis S6 hybrid: **US$630–1,099**, MOQ 1
- SolaX X1 Hybrid G4 5 kW: **US$912–1,099**, MOQ 10

Both come from **Chinese trading companies on Made-in-China.com — neither is Ginlong or
SolaX.** Before anyone treats these as our cost basis:

1. **Warranty.** Manufacturer warranty on inverters is typically tied to authorized-channel
   sale and registration. A unit bought from a trader may not register. "Honored warranty"
   is on our table-stakes list (CLAUDE.md §3) — non-negotiable, not a feature we can trade
   away for margin.
2. **The saving is smaller than it looks.** US$630–1,099 is roughly ₱35k–62k at ~₱56/$
   before freight, insurance, 12% VAT, customs duty, brokerage and inland haulage. Against
   a PH street range of ₱28k–85k for a hybrid **[S]**, the landed advantage may be near
   zero on single units — and it comes with the RMA risk.
3. **Counterfeits are documented on Pylontech specifically.** Chinese listings openly sell
   cells "to replace US2000/US3000/US5000." One dead grey-market battery on a customer's
   wall in a scam-wary Facebook market costs more than every peso this route saves
   (CLAUDE.md §1.6).
4. **MOQ 10 on SolaX** means the "cheap" price requires ~₱500k+ of inventory — which is a
   Founder-OS gate issue in its own right (§8).

**Recommendation: buy through the authorized PH channel for the first five installs.** Ask
for installer/dealer pricing, not retail. Revisit direct import only when volume makes a
container sensible *and* the brand will sell to us directly.

## 7. Partnership routes — what's actually available

The four brands sit at very different distances from a partnership conversation.

### Tier 1 — available now, costs nothing but a phone call

**Installer / dealer account with an existing PH distributor.** Photonergy (Dyness), IAN
Solar (multi-brand), Semper/PhilSolar (Pylontech), Solaric (Solis, SolaX). Typical asks:
business registration, a first order, sometimes a technical onboarding. Gets us trade
pricing instead of retail and keeps warranty intact. **This is the only tier we should be
touching right now.**

### Tier 2 — available after a track record

**Direct dealer/distributor account with the brand.** Solis is the realistic candidate
because they have a PH entity — approach via phservice@solisinverters.com for the local
route and sales@ginlong.com for commercial. Dyness has a structured application form
(partnership types: Distributor / Brand / Solution Services / Other) and 61 named global
distributor partners with visible tiers **[V]**.

Standard channel agreements in this industry cover territory rights, **annual purchase
commitments**, MOQ, pricing tiers, warranty responsibility, lead-sharing, and growth
targets **[S — industry-general, not brand-specific]**. Brand-specific numbers are all
**[U]** and only come out in a real conversation. Note that SolaX's public commercial
attention is on GWh-scale partners (the 1 GWh Raystech deal), so a PH startup is not on
their radar at this stage.

### Tier 3 — "manufacturing partnership" / OEM white-label — **not available to us yet**

What the user asked about specifically. The honest picture:

- **Dyness is the only one of the four with a visible OEM door.** "Brand" is a selectable
  partnership type on their partner application, which is the standard euphemism for
  white-label supply **[V]**. They also do co-certification deals with inverter brands
  (Sol-Ark) **[V]**, so they are partnership-minded.
- **Pylontech publishes nothing** about OEM/ODM. Their brand equity *is* the product; a
  MACC-badged Pylontech would cannibalize it. Low probability.
- **Solis and SolaX** run brand-led channel strategies. No published OEM program.
- **Industry-typical OEM economics [S, general]:** white-label with existing tooling runs
  ~10–50 units MOQ at the low end; true OEM with custom casing/branding/packaging runs
  **500–1,000 units**. At even ₱40k/unit that is ₱20M–40M of committed inventory.
- **The realistic near-term substitute is not OEM at all** — it is what SC-05 already
  says: buy the certified electronics, fabricate the racking, harnesses, cabinets and
  power boxes ourselves, and put our brand on those. Same customer-visible branding, none
  of the capital risk, and it is already un-gated (ops/status.md §2).

**A credible OEM approach needs a number we do not have.** Nobody at Dyness will discuss
white-label with a company at zero installs. The thing that makes that call work is
"we placed N units last year through your PH distributor" — which is a Tier 1 activity.

## 8. Founder-OS check (CLAUDE.md)

**The pricing and locations half of this request is squarely on the Critical Path** and
needed no gate: we cannot target-cost the ₱99,500 package (§3, price-from-the-corridor)
without knowing what the inverter and battery actually land at. Done.

**The "manufacturing partnership" half trips two rules, and I'm flagging rather than
blocking** — the research is delivered in full above, but the *action* has a gate:

| Rule | How it applies |
|---|---|
| **§7 — "Model B fabrication tooling/inventory spend before the Mission is met"** | An OEM/white-label commitment is inventory spend of the purest kind — 500–1,000 units per industry norms. Ledger status (ops/status.md, 2026-07-19): **0 paid deposits, 0 installs, Mission UNMET (0/5)**. |
| **§1.5 — never bet >1/3 of cash on a single unvalidated commitment** | Cash on hand is currently **TBD** in the ledger. With the denominator unknown, any container-scale commitment is unassessable, and §0 says an unknown number means the gate is treated as UNMET. |

**Compliant alternative (§7 requires me to name one):** run the Tier 1 ladder. Open dealer
accounts, buy at trade price for installs 1–5, log the units, and let the volume earn the
Tier 2 conversation. Model B's own branding ambition is served meanwhile by SC-05's
fabricated racking/boxes/harnesses, which are already un-gated.

**No override is being requested or logged** — this is research, not spend. If the founders
want to pursue an OEM conversation anyway, that is their call to make explicitly, and it
gets logged in `ops/status.md` §4 per §0 precedence.

## 10. Bottom line

Build the first flagship BOM on **Solis (inverter) + Dyness (battery)**, both through the
authorized PH channel:

- Solis because it is the only brand of the four with a Philippine office and a Philippine
  service number — local warranty is a table stake, not a nice-to-have.
- Dyness because Photonergy is a named, reachable, official PH distributor with a street
  address, and the pricing reputation is at or below Pylontech.
- Hold **Pylontech** as the credibility upgrade for a PLUS-tier or a customer who asks for
  it by name, and **SolaX** as the inverter fallback if Solis PH pricing disappoints.
- Treat every China-direct price in this document as a negotiating reference, not a
  sourcing plan.

No partnership conversation with any of the four is worth founder hours until the call list
in §9 is done and the first installs are banked.
