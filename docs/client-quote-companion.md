## 2. How you save money

Savings come from two places (on-grid packages):

1. **Self-use (biggest win)** — daytime solar that your home uses instead of buying from BILECO. That avoids roughly the full retail rate on your bill.
2. **Net-metering credits (smaller win)** — leftover power exported to BILECO earns **bill credits**, not cash. The credit rate is BILECO’s **blended generation cost**, which is usually less than the full retail rate.

We size systems so most power is used in the home first. The goal is a **lower monthly bill**, not “selling electricity to the co-op.”

Exact ₱/month savings depend on your recent bills, roof, and how you use power during the day. The figure on your quote is an estimate for **your** home — ask us if anything looks unclear.

## 3. Government incentives for homeowners (Philippines)

There is **no Biliran-only cash rebate** that pays part of a home solar install. What exists is **national** policy under the Renewable Energy Act (**RA 9513**), applied locally through **BILECO**.

### What helps a homeowner

| Program | What you get |
|---|---|
| **Net metering** | Systems up to **100 kW** can export excess power to BILECO and receive **peso credits** on your next bills. This is the main consumer incentive. |
| **Faster processing (2025–2026 reforms)** | Distribution utilities / electric cooperatives are directed to process complete net-metering applications on a short statutory clock (around **10 working days**). LGUs also have tighter permit / inspection timelines. |
| **REC ownership** | Recent DOE policy: you may **own the Renewable Energy Certificates** from the clean power you generate. This is a real right; peso value at home scale is still developing. |

### What usually does **not** apply to a home rooftop

- **Feed-in Tariff (FIT)** — for large power plants, not typical home systems.
- **Income tax holiday / BOI developer perks** — for renewable-energy companies and projects, not ordinary household buyers.
- **A provincial cash subsidy** — none confirmed for Biliran residential solar as of this note.

If a seller promises “government will refund half your install,” ask for the exact law and agency letter. We do not make that claim.

## 4. Permits, BILECO & what we handle

For on-grid / hybrid installs that use net metering, the usual path is:

1. Site survey and final design for your roof  
2. LGU electrical permit  
3. Install and testing  
4. LGU final electrical inspection (CFEI)  
5. BILECO net-metering application  
6. Bidirectional meter swap and signed net-metering agreement  

**MACC’s job:** prepare documents, coordinate the licensed electrical sign-off where required, file with BILECO, and walk the paperwork with you — so you are not left chasing offices alone.

**BILECO’s job:** review the application, schedule the meter change, and apply export credits on your bill once the agreement is live.

Timelines depend on a complete application and BILECO / LGU schedules. We quote the legal processing windows honestly and update you if a step moves.

## 7. Quick FAQs

**Will my bill go to zero?**  
Usually no. Night use, cloudy days, and fixed charges remain. Good systems cut a large share of daytime grid use; we show a realistic range, not a zero-bill promise.

**Do I get cash from BILECO?**  
No. Net metering gives **bill credits**, not a payout.

**Is there a government rebate for Biliran homes?**  
No confirmed cash rebate. Net metering and faster permitting are the real programs.

**What if I only care about brownouts?**  
Choose **ILAW** or **SANDIGAN**. A portable power station can also cover a few appliances but does **not** lower your BILECO bill or qualify for net metering the way a roof system can.

**Who do I call after install?**  
MACC — local team. Warranty and service contacts are on your handover pack.
